Vertical axis wind turbine

Personal project

A drag-type Savonius rotor I'm designing and building myself. Savonius turbines are slow and simple, but they start in light, gusty wind from any direction, which makes them a good fit for a small battery-charging setup.

Most of the work so far has been analysis: estimating the startup torque the rotor needs to get moving, and the gap between peak output and what it averages over real wind. Peak power is around 500 W; the average is closer to 10 W, and that gap drives most of the electrical decisions.

Rotor
Savonius, drag type
Output
~500 W peak, ~10 W average
System
24 V, LiFePO4 battery
Charging
Commercial wind charge controller
Monitoring
Rectifier, voltage and current displays, microcontroller
Status
Choosing the generator

VectorCam

Hardware team, Dr. Acharya's lab, Johns Hopkins CBID

VectorCam is an AI-enabled device for mosquito vector surveillance: it images collected mosquitoes so they can be identified quickly in the field, without a trained specialist at a microscope. Knowing which species are present, and where, is what lets health teams target malaria and other mosquito-borne disease.

I'm on the hardware side of the team, working with Dr. Acharya's lab at the Johns Hopkins Center for Bioengineering Innovation and Design, and I help bring new mechanical engineering undergrads onto the hardware team.

AI-enabled mosquito surveillance device
Where
Johns Hopkins CBID, Dr. Acharya's lab
