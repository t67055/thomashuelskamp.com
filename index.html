VectorCam 2

Vector Control Innovations, August 2026 – November 2026

VectorCam 2 is the first universal device for phone-based vector surveillance. It pairs any smartphone with a magnifying glass and LEDs to image and identify mosquitoes in the field — work that was previously limited to trained entomologists using microscopes. Knowing which species are present, and where, enables communities to target malaria and other mosquito-borne disease.

Previous generations of VectorCam devices served as the foundation for building VCI's mosquito database. But they were also phone-specific, adding unnecessary barriers of time and money to reaching democratized vector surveillance.

VectorCam 2 builds on the footprint of past devices, but is adaptable to any phone, cuts out unnecessary electronics, and is designed to be manufactured anywhere in the world.

Bioinspired Turbine

Senior Design, August 2026 – Present

Our team was tasked with testing and building a novel vertical axis Savonius wind turbine. Based on preliminary work by Dr. Rajat Mittal (JHU), our turbine implements diodic baffles and perforations. These features work like feathers on a bird's wing, reducing drag during the recovery portion of each revolution.

We started with a scaled-down turbine, running experiments on baffle shape, size, and orientation in a wind tunnel.

Next, we used our optimized turbine configuration to design and build a full-scale, 2-foot turbine. Our final turbine powers an array of environmental monitors: temperature, humidity, and air quality.

Global Manufacturing

Vector Control Innovations, January 2026 – Present

What does it take to bring a device from prototype to product?

Scaling up VCI's manufacturing has meant three ongoing efforts:

  1. In-house production: building and running our own 3D print farm.
  2. Local manufacturing: working with Kenyan manufacturers to produce devices at scale.
  3. Open-source hardware: publishing the designs and a step-by-step build guide at build-vectorcam.org.

Underneath all three are the systems that keep production consistent: manufacturing specs, a device ID protocol, specimen label printing, and the documentation that ties them together.

VectorCam Tablet

Vector Control Innovations, January 2026 – March 2026

The VectorCam Tablet prototypes were built for Kenya's Ministry of Health and served as platforms for experimenting with both VectorCam's form and function.

Their design began with the question, “What if VectorCam were treated as a single device instead of a hardware add-on?” To support this idea, tablets are integrated (but still removable) into each device.

This project also started to investigate design for printing and design for assembly. In order to print the large, thin cases, prints were balanced at a 45° angle. In addition, it uses a consolidated, universal electronics assembly designed by Santiago (VCI).

The considerations of manufacturing, assembly, and consolidated electronics were built on in VectorCam 2.
